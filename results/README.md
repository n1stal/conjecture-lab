# Lean-checked results

The default build checks the claims below. [Palomar registrations](../docs/palomar-registrations.md) link to the source commits checked by the external registry.

| Result | Kind | What the proof establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [A counterexample to Turau’s amnesiac flooding conjecture](amnesiac-flooding/README.md) | Counterexample | For each k ≥ 4, a connected non-bipartite graph on 6k vertices with radius floor(5k/2) floods in at most three rounds from k sources, violating Turau’s conjectured lower bound. |
| [Ordered spectral proximity forces adjacency rank at most five](spectral-rank-problem-21/README.md) | Answer to a problem | For any simple graph on 3n vertices, n ≥ 3, squared distance of its ordered adjacency spectrum to (2n, 0, …, 0, −n, −n) strictly below 2/3 forces real adjacency rank at most five. This answers Problem 21, not Conjecture 20. |
| [Inverse Z-polynomials through rank four](inverse-z-rank-four/README.md) | Partial theorem | Positive coefficients through the rank and log-concavity for matroids on finite ambient types of rank at most four. The unrestricted conjecture remains open here. |
| [Strict inverse Z log-concavity for line-preserving projective restrictions](inverse-z-projective/README.md) | Partial theorem | In every rank, the inverse Z-polynomial of a finite projective geometry restricted to points retaining at least two on every line has positive coefficients through rank and strict log-concavity at every interior degree. The unrestricted conjecture is not settled. |
| [Counterexamples to Van Cott–Zhang Conjecture 5.2(1) and (3)](van-cott-zhang-521/README.md) | Counterexample | The prefix pattern exists at every length at least 11. Counterexamples to the general prohibition occur at every length 13, 16, 19, …, and to the D/G classification at every length 14, 17, 20, …. The family theorem covers lengths 11 and above. |
| [Property P: the finite witness {1, 2}](property-p-baseline/audit.md) | Known baseline | The set {1, 2} has Property P and its reciprocal sum is 3/2. This is not a search certificate or an upper bound. |
